# Arabic translation brief — kinē

**For:** a translator with medical or physiotherapy terminology experience,
Gulf/MSA Arabic
**Deliverable:** one filled column in `translation.csv`
**Scope:** roughly 400 UI strings plus 136 exercise names plus 28 presentation
education passages

## What the app is

A self-management physiotherapy app for the UAE. A person marks where it hurts,
answers seven questions, and follows a short daily exercise programme. Arabic is a
first-class language here, not an afterthought — about half the intended audience
will use it.

## The file

`docs/handoff/translation.csv`, generated by `scripts/i18n-export.mjs`:

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `key` | do not change |
| `english` | source |
| `current_arabic` | what ships today — may be good, generic, or empty |
| `status` | `missing`, `interim`, or `ok` |
| `context` | which screen, and whether the string is safety-critical |
| `max_chars` | the layout's comfortable limit for this string |

Fill a new `arabic` column. Leave `key` untouched.

**`status` tells you where to spend effort:**

- `missing` — nothing exists. Translate.
- `interim` — a generic placeholder is standing in for specific content. **These
  matter most.** For example, every exercise currently shows the same three generic
  Arabic cues regardless of which exercise it is, and several distinct English
  clinical notices share one Arabic sentence.
- `ok` — a real translation exists; please still review it.

## Voice

The English is deliberately calm, plain and non-clinical in tone even when the content
is clinical. It avoids hospital language and avoids urgency except where urgency is
the point. Representative lines:

- "A little movement. A good day."
- "No catching up. No falling behind. Just your next small step."
- "Better is a direction. Not a deadline."
- "Your response matters more than the calendar."
- "Earned, not scheduled."

Please carry that register into Arabic. Warm, direct, second person, not formal
institutional Arabic and not marketing Arabic. Where a literal translation would land
as cold or clinical, prefer a natural Arabic phrasing that keeps the feeling — note
any such choice in a comment column so we can check it.

Gulf-comprehensible MSA. Where a clinical term has both a common and a formal Arabic
form, prefer the one a patient in Dubai would understand, and put the formal term in
brackets on first use if it helps.

## Three rules that are not negotiable

1. **Safety strings are literal.** Anything marked safety-critical in `context` —
   red-flag warnings, the urgent-assessment messages, the pain-limit guidance — must
   carry exactly the same meaning and the same level of urgency. No softening to sound
   gentler. The chest-pain and cauda-equina messages in particular must read as
   "seek emergency assessment now", not as advice.
2. **Numbers and units stay recognisable.** The app uses a 0–10 pain scale and
   "≤ 4/10", "within 24 hours". Keep the numerals as digits. If you use Arabic-Indic
   numerals (٤/١٠), be consistent across every string — tell us which convention you
   chose and we will match the rest of the interface to it.
3. **Respect `max_chars` where you can.** The interface is built for phones. A label
   with room for 18 characters cannot take 40. Where Arabic genuinely needs more room,
   flag the row rather than truncating the meaning — we will adjust the layout.

## Terminology to settle first

Please give us your preferred Arabic for these before doing the bulk, so they stay
consistent throughout. We will lock them into a glossary:

pain · discomfort · flare-up · irritability · load · loading · mobility · isometric ·
hold · reps · sets · tempo · range of motion · phase · Calm / Load / Capacity (the
three programme phases) · pattern match · red flag · check-in · streak · aggravator ·
easer · nerve symptoms · numbness · tingling · weakness · stiffness · assessment ·
programme · session · dose

"Calm", "Load" and "Capacity" are the three phase names and appear as short labels
everywhere — they need Arabic that works at badge length.

## What not to translate

- Anything in `src/data/*.json` other than what appears in the CSV — the export already
  pulls the fields that need you.
- The brand name **kinē**. It stays Latin script.
- The 3D body map's anatomical orientation labels (front/back) are handled separately.

## Notes on direction

The interface mirrors for Arabic (right-to-left) automatically. The 3D body does
**not** mirror — a mirrored body would misreport which side hurts. So "your left
shoulder" always means the person's actual left. Keep left/right references literal.

## Delivery

The filled CSV back to us. We paste the column, clear the `interim` flags, and re-run
the layout screenshots. Expect one round of "this string now overflows" fixes — that
is normal and quick.

If any English source string is ambiguous, unclear, or itself badly written, say so.
Several were written fast and would benefit from a second pass in both languages.
